## How to read these results

- **All data is synthetic.** The generator and the parser were written together, so 100% on clean text PDFs mostly shows that the parser reads the format the generator writes. Real supplier quotations will have wrapped descriptions, multi-page tables, different column orders, GST lines and discounts, none of which are handled or tested here.
- **Price checks are conservative by design.** A rate is flagged only when it is more than 1.3x (or less than 0.7x) of the historical median and more than 3.5 robust deviations away. In hard mode the injected spikes are 1.1x to 1.5x, so many sit below the threshold: 46% of high and 27% of low deviations were caught. Overpricing of 10 to 30 percent is not flagged. Lowering the threshold would catch more and raise false alarms; the clean-item false flag rate in hard mode is 0.8%.
- **Scans are the weak spot.** Only 58.6% of line items were recovered with exactly the right quantity, rate and amount, and 18.4% of the items the parser did produce (30 of 163) were wrong (I did not break the errors down by type; misread digits are the likely cause). This is why every OCR document is sent to review and why OCR digits cannot be trusted without a person comparing them with the image.
- **Small samples.** The scan runs contain 7 high, 3 low and 2 arithmetic anomalies. Treat those 100% figures as "no misses in a handful", not as a rate.
- **OCR upscale factor.** The 1.5x upscale was chosen by comparing 1.0x, 1.5x and 2.0x on a separate set of 8 scanned documents (22, 37 and 37 of 51 items recovered exactly). It was not tuned on the 32 documents above, but it was tuned on the same generator.
- **Catalog matching** is 99.2% on noisy descriptions among recovered items and 94.7% among recovered scan items.

## What would make this a real evaluation

Collect 100 or more real quotations from several suppliers (with permission), label the line items by hand, measure extraction on a held-out split, and replace the synthetic price history with real purchase prices.
